For most people in Africa, the flood warning is the water itself.

Forewarn warns you days before a flood reaches your area: your street, your farm, your village. Free, anywhere in Africa, no account needed.

How Forewarn warns you

  • Ask in plain words. Put a pin on your door and ask if it will flood. The answer gives a level (unlikely, possible or likely) and the chance in words, such as "about 1 in 4 forecasts".
  • Speak your question. Tap the microphone and ask out loud in English, French or Swahili. Meta's Muse Voice Transcribe writes it down, and you can fix the words before you send. Each language was tested on recordings of native speakers before it was turned on.
  • 14 days ahead. 51 runs of the European (ECMWF) forecast for your spot, counted for you.
  • How wet the ground is. The last 30 days of rain against 30 years of records. Soaked ground floods sooner.
  • The last flood in 3D. Lagos and Nairobi, street by street, with the water at each door.
  • Send a photo of the water. Meta's SAM 3.1 marks the water and the people, Muse Spark says what the water reaches, and people are pixelated.
  • Alerts a person approves. An agent checks your area every 6 hours and drafts an email alert when flooding looks possible. A person approves it before it goes out.
  • Warn your family. The agent writes a short message, and you send it on WhatsApp from your own phone.

Every number has a source

Code computes every number, and a checker compares each number the agent says with the data. The agent runs on Meta's Muse Spark Open.

We replayed 36 past African floods with today's rules: 21 got a warning, 19 of them 3 days or more ahead, and 15 were missed. See every flood in the time machine.

Forewarn is not an official warning service. If your local officials say leave, leave.
